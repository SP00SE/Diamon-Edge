STREAK LABELS

Compares a player's last 7 games batting average (L7 AVG) to their full season AVG. The difference is measured in batting average points.

🔥 Hot StreakL7 AVG is 30+ points higher than season AVG — player is on fire recently
❄️ Cold StreakL7 AVG is 30+ points lower than season AVG — player is in a recent slump
— SteadyL7 AVG is within 29 points of season AVG — performing close to their normal level

Example: Season AVG .280, L7 AVG .220 → difference = −60 pts → ❄️ Cold Streak

Hot Streaks Tab — Flame Intensity shown on streak cards

The number of 🔥 (hits) or 💥 (HRs) on a streak card shows how hot the streak is.

📊 Stats Glossary ▶

BATTER STATS

AVG Batting Average

Hits divided by at-bats. The most fundamental measure of hitting ability. MLB average is typically around .250.

.300+Elite hitter
.270–.299Above average
<.250Below average — struggles to convert contact
OBP On-Base Percentage

How often a batter reaches base — hits, walks, and hit-by-pitch combined. A better measure of offensive value than AVG alone.

.370+Elite plate discipline
.320–.369Average to above average
<.320Below average — limited ability to avoid outs
SLG Slugging Percentage

Total bases divided by at-bats — a single = 1, double = 2, triple = 3, home run = 4. Measures raw power production.

.550+Elite slugger
.400–.479Average
<.300Weak power profile
ISO Isolated Power (SLG − AVG)

Pure power metric — strips out singles to isolate extra-base-hit ability. Used in the HR Score to identify true power hitters independent of batting average.

.270+Elite raw power
.170–.219Average power
<.090No power — contact/slap hitter profile
BABIP Batting Average on Balls In Play

How often a batter gets a hit when they put the ball in play — home runs and strikeouts excluded. League average is around .290–.300. One of the best indicators of whether results reflect skill or luck.

.330+Elite — balls in play becoming hits at an exceptional rate
.290–.329Average — results in line with league norms
<.265Below average — balls turning into outs more than expected

Example: A batter hitting .220 with a BABIP of .340 is likely due for positive regression — they're making solid contact but the balls aren't falling in yet.

BACON Batting Average on CONtact

Similar to BABIP but includes home runs — measures how often a batter gets a hit on any contact (strikeouts excluded). A high BACON means the batter consistently converts contact into hits.

.320+Elite — almost every ball in play is a threat
.280–.319Average — solid contact, no major red flags
<.265Below average — contact not converting
GHP Games with Hit Percentage

The percentage of games in which a player records at least one hit. A consistency metric popular in Beat the Streak — a player who hits in 70%+ of games is far more reliable than one who goes hitless half the time.

≥ 70%Elite — hits in 7 out of every 10 games
55–69%Average — solid but not fully consistent
< 55%Inconsistent — frequent hitless games, risky for streak play

Shown on The Lab hitter card and factors into the Lab matchup score (up to ±7 pts).

K% (Batter) Batter Strikeout Rate

Percentage of plate appearances that end in a strikeout. Lower is better — elite contact hitters rarely strike out.

< 10%Elite contact hitter (Arraez-tier)
10–18%Good contact rate
19–24%League average
> 25%High strikeout rate — risky for streak play

PITCHER STATS

ERA Earned Run Average

Earned runs allowed per 9 innings. The most common measure of pitcher quality — lower is better. MLB average is around 4.00–4.20.

≤ 2.50Elite ace — extremely tough to score against
2.51–3.30Strong — above-average run prevention
3.31–4.20Average
≥ 5.00Weak — prone to giving up runs
≥ 5.50Very weak — high-value target for hitters
WHIP Walks + Hits per Inning Pitched

How many baserunners a pitcher allows per inning. A tighter measure of control and contact allowed than ERA alone. MLB average is around 1.25–1.30.

≤ 0.95Elite — almost no baserunners
0.96–1.10Strong command
1.11–1.35Average
≥ 1.50Weak — lots of traffic on the bases
BB/BF% Walk Rate per Batter Faced

How often a pitcher walks a batter out of every batter they face. A high BB/BF% signals poor command — a direct advantage for hitters.

< 6%Elite command — rarely walks anyone
7–11%Average to below-average command
> 12%Wild — hitters benefit from frequent free passes

Used in The Lab matchup score: high BB/BF% adds up to +6 pts for the hitter; elite command subtracts up to −6 pts.

K% (Pitcher) Pitcher Strikeout Rate

Percentage of batters faced that end in a strikeout. High K% signals elite swing-and-miss stuff — difficult matchup for any hitter.

≥ 29%Elite swing-and-miss — tough matchup for any hitter
25–28%Above average K stuff
21–24%League average
< 16%Contact-prone — hitters put the ball in play regularly
HR/9 Home Runs Allowed per 9 Innings

How many home runs a pitcher surrenders per 9 innings. A high HR/9 signals a fly-ball pitcher with poor HR suppression — a premium target for HR picks.

≥ 1.50Very HR-prone — high-value target for power hitters
1.20–1.49HR-prone — above-average HR risk
0.90–1.19Average HR rate allowed
< 0.40HR suppressor — elite fly-ball control
P-BABIP BABIP Allowed by Pitcher

How often batters get a hit when making contact against this pitcher (home runs excluded). A high P-BABIP means batters are putting balls in play and getting hits — the pitcher doesn't generate weak contact.

≥ .330Allows lots of hits on contact — hitter-friendly matchup
.281–.309Average contact allowed
≤ .260Induces weak contact — tough matchup
P-BACON BACON Allowed by Pitcher

Same as P-BABIP but includes home runs — the full picture of how often batters convert contact into hits against this pitcher. A high P-BACON means the pitcher allows hard, productive contact.

≥ .310Hitters make hard contact — dangerous for pitchers
.271–.309Average contact quality allowed
≤ .250Elite contact suppressor — limits hard contact effectively
